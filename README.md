# Customer Behaviour & Marketing Campaign Analysis

**Excel Data Analytics Portfolio Project**  
*Customer Segmentation | Purchasing Behaviour | Marketing Performance*

---

## Project Overview

**How do customer characteristics relate to purchasing behaviour, and what can businesses learn from marketing campaign responses?**

This project explores customer purchasing behaviour and marketing campaign engagement using Microsoft Excel.

The objective was to identify spending patterns, examine relationships between customer income and expenditure, and investigate opportunities for more effective customer segmentation.

The analysis uses an educational dataset adapted from an iFood recruitment case study, containing **2,021 customer records**.

**Project context:** Completed as part of a guided Data Analytics Accelerator (DAA) learning programme.

## Key Findings

### 01 — Higher-Income Customers Generally Spend More

The analysis identified a positive association between customer income and total spending.

However, substantial variation exists among customers with similar incomes, suggesting that **income alone is insufficient to explain purchasing behaviour**.

**Business implication:** Combining income with purchasing history and other customer characteristics may provide more useful customer segmentation than relying on income alone.

### 02 — Campaign 6 Respondents Have Higher Average Spending

| Customer group | Customers | Average spending |
|---|---:|---:|
| All customers | 2,021 | 563.79 |
| Campaign 6 respondents | 311 | 917.26 |

Customers who responded to Campaign 6 had substantially higher average spending than the overall customer population.

However, this comparison does not establish that the campaign increased spending. Respondents may already have been more valuable or engaged customers before the campaign.

**Business implication:** Investigate which customer characteristics predict campaign response, and use appropriate control groups to evaluate whether future campaigns generate incremental value.

### 03 — Customer Spending Is Unevenly Distributed

The spending distribution is positively skewed, with a relatively small group of high-spending customers and a larger concentration of customers at lower expenditure levels.

**Business implication:** Businesses could explore customer-value segments and investigate whether different groups respond to different marketing approaches.

## Tools & Methodology

**Primary tool: Microsoft Excel**

The analytical workflow involved:

- **Data exploration:** Examining customer characteristics, purchasing behaviour and campaign engagement.
- **Descriptive statistics:** Calculating customer spending and campaign response summaries.
- **Relationship analysis:** Creating XY scatter plots to investigate income and spending.
- **Distribution analysis:** Using histograms to examine variation in customer expenditure.
- **Group comparisons:** Comparing Campaign 6 respondents with the overall customer population.
- **Business interpretation:** Translating descriptive findings into potential marketing applications.

---

## Business Recommendations

Based on the exploratory findings, three areas warrant further investigation.

**1. Develop more informative customer segments**

Combine income, purchasing history, age and campaign engagement to understand differences in customer behaviour.

**2. Improve campaign targeting analysis**

Examine which customer groups have higher campaign response rates and whether those groups also demonstrate higher customer value.

**3. Evaluate incremental campaign impact**

Use randomised control groups or suitable comparison methods to distinguish existing customer differences from genuine campaign effects.

*These recommendations represent opportunities for further analysis rather than proven improvements in marketing performance.*

---

## Limitations

This is an exploratory, observational analysis and **does not establish causal relationships**.

Key limitations include:

- Campaign respondents may differ systematically from non-respondents.
- Higher average spending among respondents does not demonstrate campaign effectiveness.
- Income and spending refer to different reporting periods.
- Monetary units in the source dataset have not been independently confirmed.
- The educational dataset may not fully represent real-world customer populations.

## Project Takeaway

This project demonstrates how accessible analytical tools such as Excel can be used to explore customer behaviour and identify commercially relevant questions.

The central learning is that **identifying a relationship is different from demonstrating its cause**.

Turning descriptive insights into effective marketing decisions requires careful interpretation, appropriate segmentation and further testing.
